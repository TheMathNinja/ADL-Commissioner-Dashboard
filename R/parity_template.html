Definitions, coverage and limitations

H2H columns count only actual head-to-head games. ADL Reg Season adds Bonus Games reconstructed using the local playoff-picture rules: rank cumulative all-play results, then points, then potential points for weeks 1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12, and 1–12. Each event gives ranks 1–15 a win, 16–17 a tie, and 18–32 a loss. Only completed events count. Byes and postseason games are excluded. Bonus outcomes are correlated with scoring, so the coin-flip baseline is descriptive rather than a schedule model. ADL’s regular season is weeks 1–12, as defined in the existing playoff-picture script; historical MFL metadata incorrectly marks later contests as regular season and is overridden. Identical duplicate matchups count once; conflicting duplicates stop the build. NFL games without final scores, including the canceled 2022 Buffalo–Cincinnati game, are excluded.

Record spread is the population standard deviation of franchise winning percentages. The coin-flip baseline is √mean(0.25 ÷ games played), a conventional independent-game approximation rather than an exact schedule simulation. A ratio near 1 indicates about as much spread as this baseline; larger values indicate more imbalance. Ties count as half a win, with the .25 baseline retained as an approximation. Each team is equally weighted.

Normalized net-margin spread is the standard deviation across teams of their average signed margin divided by average league team-game points. Game-margin metrics use the same league-season scale. The close (≤25%) and lopsided (≥50%) thresholds are descriptive, not NFL possession rules. These ratios remove differences in score units but not every structural difference between NFL and fantasy scoring.

Yearly β is an ordinary least-squares slope with an intercept. Pooled record β first centers each league-season at its team-average winning percentage, then pools completed year pairs. Normalized net-margin β is also centered within each season before pooling. Gap reduction (1 − β) describes the fitted reduction in above/below-average separation; it is not the share of franchises that improved. Correlation measures association, not causation. Persistence and regression both reflect skill, roster turnover, management changes, schedule and sampling luck; they do not identify a scoring-rule effect.

Bottom/top quartiles contain eight team-equivalents in a 32-team league. Teams tied at a cutoff share membership rather than being separated alphabetically. “Winning” means strictly above .500 and “losing” strictly below .500.
