Parity

How balanced are records and scores, and how quickly do stronger and weaker teams change places?

Compare NFL weeks 1–12 with ADL H2H, and NFL weeks 1–17 with ADL Reg Season. NFL byes mean these windows usually contain 11 and 16 games per team. ADL H2H includes head-to-head games only. ADL Reg Season includes H2H plus Bonus Games. Completed seasons have 12 H2H games and 5 Bonus Games per franchise; season-to-date includes only Bonus Games already completed. Scoring margins remain H2H-only because Bonus Games have no opponent score margin.

Rank each team’s total score among teams that played that week, then compare the opponents’ scoring percentiles. Close = within 10 percentile points (about 3 places in a 32-team ranking); lopsided = at least 50 percentile points apart (about 16 places). This adjusts for the spread and shape of each league’s scores. It measures similarity in whole-game performance, not whether a game stayed competitive or was decided by one possession.

Definitions, coverage and limitations

H2H columns count only actual head-to-head games. ADL Reg Season adds Bonus Games reconstructed using the local playoff-picture rules: rank cumulative all-play results, then points, then potential points for weeks 1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12, and 1–12. Each event gives ranks 1–15 a win, 16–17 a tie, and 18–32 a loss. Only completed events count. Byes and postseason games are excluded. Bonus outcomes are correlated with scoring, so the coin-flip baseline is descriptive rather than a schedule model. ADL’s regular season is weeks 1–12, as defined in the existing playoff-picture script; historical MFL metadata incorrectly marks later contests as regular season and is overridden. Identical duplicate matchups count once; conflicting duplicates stop the build. NFL games without final scores, including the canceled 2022 Buffalo–Cincinnati game, are excluded.

Completed-season ranges are inclusive. Record distributions pool equally weighted team-seasons; record and net-margin spread pool within-season squared deviations, rather than measuring the spread of multi-year franchise records. Top–bottom gaps average season-specific quartile gaps. Performance-gap shares pool games, and random-pairing ratios are calculated from pooled observed and expected shares. Mobility uses adjacent pairs with both years inside the range. Recovery follows the first selected season’s quartiles through the final selected season. A one-year range has no mobility or recovery interval. Downloads retain the underlying year-specific data.

Record spread is the population standard deviation of franchise winning percentages. The coin-flip baseline is √mean(0.25 ÷ games played), a conventional independent-game approximation rather than an exact schedule simulation. A ratio near 1 indicates about as much spread as this baseline; larger values indicate more imbalance. Ties count as half a win, with the .25 baseline retained as an approximation. Each team is equally weighted.

Normalized net-margin spread is the standard deviation across teams of their average signed margin divided by average league team-game points. Game-margin metrics use the same league-season scale. Mean-normalized margin measures remove score units but do not adjust for scoring dispersion. Close/lopsided performance metrics instead compare midrank scoring percentiles within each week (tied scores share their rank; the distinct lowest and highest scores are 0 and 100). Close is a gap ≤10 percentile points, lopsided ≥50. Random-pairing indices divide the observed share by the share across all possible pairs of that week’s participating teams, weighted by actual weekly game counts. This also controls for byes and score ties. These are rank-based whole-game performance comparisons, not possession-based competitiveness measures.

Yearly β is an ordinary least-squares slope with an intercept. Pooled record β first centers each league-season at its team-average winning percentage, then pools completed year pairs. Normalized net-margin β is also centered within each season before pooling. Gap reduction (1 − β) describes the fitted reduction in above/below-average separation; it is not the share of franchises that improved. Correlation measures association, not causation. Persistence and regression both reflect skill, roster turnover, management changes, schedule and sampling luck; they do not identify a scoring-rule effect.

Bottom/top quartiles contain eight team-equivalents in a 32-team league. Teams tied at a cutoff share membership rather than being separated alphabetically. “Winning” means strictly above .500 and “losing” strictly below .500. Franchise IDs track ADL teams across years; NFL team codes track NFL franchises. Ownership changes are not isolated. A 2026 partial-year comparison has fewer games and more noise, and never enters the completed-season pool.
